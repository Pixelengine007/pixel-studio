# PocketForge lessons for Pixel Studio

Source: the 142 PocketForge reviews (July–November 2026) in the Claude Project "PIXEL STUDIO".

**Owner decision (23 Nov 2026):** Thomas's past taste and feel preferences are NOT rules. Every creative decision (genre, style, feel, palette, grid, mood) is made on research into what works best with players now, then judged by audience data. The taste notes at the bottom are background only.

## Process lessons (facts, these do apply)

1. **Build games, not systems.** PocketForge spent most of its effort on engines, foundries, validators, toolkits and roadmaps. Build → bot test → clip → post.
2. **Real players are the judge.** In about four months and six or more games nothing was released. Publish early and let audience data decide.
3. **Self-review was overconfident.** Automated checks repeatedly "passed" games a human then rejected (1/10 to 3.5/10). Automated checks prove mechanics, never fun.
4. **Small scope wins.** The best results were one core action in one small space.
5. **Web load time matters.** Godot web builds took about 19–22 seconds to start. Use Phaser.
6. **Check before posting:** seams between surfaces, objects floating or sliding over the ground, art suggesting a path the collision blocks, mixed pixel sizes, placeholder art shown as final, an unclear goal, expensive retry.

## Background only (not rules)

- Past preferences: simple chunky pixel art on one fixed grid (320 × 180), nature themes, small animals, light in darkness, forgiving controls, readable threats, cheap retry, a calm payoff.
- Past approved concepts: Outrun the Flock (sheep herding) and Night Moth (moth escaping an owl). Candidate ideas among many; research decides.
